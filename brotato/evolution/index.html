Brotato Evolution Guide — Choose Mecha or Mutation

Choose Mecha to direct upgrades into weapons. Consider Mutation when item effects carry your run. Both directions also change enemies and their hazards.

This is our editorial framework based on the developer’s Evolution explanation. It is not a measured ranking.

What the Evolution stat does

Evolution is a Primal Dread stat. You push it toward Mecha or Mutation by picking up matching drops on the Jungle map. Mecha reinforces weapons and adds stats. Mutation copies item effects at random to multiply their value. Guides present the two paths as a choice, and this page does not claim that one run can complete both. Mecha parts can also come from dismantling mecha weapons in the shop, as the launch post describes. Guides give different threshold numbers, so read the in-game Evolution readout before planning around a specific value.

Follow the drops and watch the hazards

Mecha parts feed weapon reinforcement. The launch post also describes dismantling mecha weapons for additional parts. Mutation copies item effects randomly. Inspect the actual result before building your next shop decision around it.

Mecha enemies introduce lasers and slowing oil. Mutant enemies spread acid. Leave an exit lane instead of crossing a puddle to collect every drop. Directed upgrades can be easier to plan around, while random copies require checking what the run actually gained.

Read the branch before copying a build

The 12 November Balancing Patch brings the tested Evolution changes to the Steam default branch. Mechanization grants +3% weapon damage per mecha power point, previously +5%. At eight mechanizations a weapon deals 208% of its base damage, previously 280%. Extra piercing and bounces are removed. Stat bonuses stop growing after rank eight, and Mecha Parts drop chance falls from 2% to 1.5%. Budget crowd coverage separately. Older launch builds and the 6 and 10 November beta notes describe earlier stages of these changes. 12 November balance notes

Inspect Mutation results after the balance patch

Each mutation now puts its -1 Evolution on the Tumor. An item becomes mutant when it loses a negative modifier, and duplicate Tumor effects merge into one line. Mutation Fruit base drop chance rises from 3% to 4%, then falls by one percentage point every ten waves from wave 30, to a minimum of 1%. Inspect the Tumor when checking what a mutation changed. 12 November balance notes

Snowball after hotfix 1.2.0.3

Snowball now grants Elemental Damage only for items bought in the shop or found in crates. Tumor mutations no longer grant extra Elemental Damage on every mutation. Keep that distinction in mind when valuing another mutation for an elemental build. 1.2.0.3 hotfix notes

Optional 1.2.0.4 beta: compare drops and item limits

The 17 November open beta raises Mecha Parts drops from 1.5% to 2.5% per mecha enemy killed. Chimera Cells changes from 10% mutation chance per copy with a five-copy limit to 15% with a four-copy limit. Treat these as beta rules when planning collections and purchases; the 12 November default-branch values above remain the reference for 1.2.0.3.

This beta also keeps the Evolution damage bonus through in-run stat recalculations and fixes evolved enemy projectiles dealing only 1 damage. Leave room to dodge their normal damage rather than planning around that bug. Official 1.2.0.4 beta notes

Make the next shop decision

If you lack damage, compare an immediate weapon upgrade with reinforcement. If collection is killing you, buy enough movement or defense to reach the next wave. With Mutation, inspect copied effects rather than assuming the best passive was selected. Reassess when the actual build changes.
