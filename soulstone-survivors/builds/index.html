Build around a trigger and a payoff

Choose one damage plan, then make the rest of your slots help it happen more often or keep you alive. A fast utility skill and a slower heavy hitter can work together through a compatible skill chain; six individually attractive skills may not form that route.

The historical FreqRL example combines quick Swing skills with Arcane Beam using a Swing-to-Arcane chain. Its particular power and damage values must be checked against your installed version.

Build in an order you can recover from

  1. Read your weapon’s starting skill and available types. Pick a damage identity before spending every reroll.
  2. Take an early skill that can clear waves now. Mark the additional type or effect needed for your intended chain.
  3. When the chain or payoff becomes available, read its exact conditions. A matching tag is a candidate, not proof that every condition is satisfied.
  4. Choose upgrades that improve your actual core: repeated triggers, effect application or a damage multiplier it uses. Keep a survival option if enemies are already reaching you.
  5. At the end of the run, compare which skills dealt damage with when you became unsafe. Change one weak link rather than replacing all six slots at once.

When the planned skill does not arrive

Keep a working early damage skill until its replacement is available. If several slots already share a useful type, choose a payoff compatible with those slots instead of throwing away all of them for one rare offer. If no enabling power appears, play a straightforward first-clear route and use the run for permanent progress. These are fallback decisions, not guarantees about the random offer pool.

FreqRL’s historical model uses quick support skills to trigger a slower damaging skill. Use that relationship as a testable idea; do not copy its 2024 cooldowns or assume the same combination is the current fastest build.
